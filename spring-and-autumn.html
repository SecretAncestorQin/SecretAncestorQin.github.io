Chronicles / Spring and Autumn

770–c. 453 BCE

Spring and Autumn 春秋

With the Zhou king reduced to a figurehead, well over a hundred states competed, allied and absorbed one another. The period takes its name from the Spring and Autumn Annals, a chronicle of the state of Lu.

Dates
770–c. 453 BCE; some historians end it in 476
Royal capital
Luoyi, modern Luoyang
Leading states
Qi, Jin, Chu, Qin, Wu, Yue
Followed by
Warring States period

Protectors of the realm

No state was strong enough to replace the king, so the strongest lord of each generation acted as hegemon. He called the other lords to conferences, settled disputes and led joint armies, all in the king's name. Duke Huan of Qi, advised by his minister Guan Zhong, was the first. Duke Wen of Jin followed.

Fewer and larger

War in this period still followed aristocratic rules, fought by nobles in chariots. But the trend was clear. Large states on the edges, such as Chu in the south and Qin in the west, expanded into new land, and small states in the centre disappeared. Iron tools began to spread, and with them more farmland and more people.

The age of Confucius

Confucius lived from 551 to 479 BCE and taught that order would return if rulers and subjects went back to the duties and rituals of the early Zhou. He found no ruler willing to employ him for long. Tradition places Laozi and the strategist Sun Tzu in the same period, though the dates of both are uncertain.
